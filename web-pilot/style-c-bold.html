Order of Operations

When a problem has more than one operation, you cannot just work left to right. There is an order you must follow.

Why the order matters

Look at 5 + 3 × 2.

If you go left to right you get 16. That answer is wrong.

Multiply first, then add, and you get 11. That is the right answer. Everyone has to use the same order or we would all get different answers.

The order

The four stages of the order of operations, in order.
Do thisSignWhat it means
1st( )Anything inside parentheses. Finish it completely first.
2nd4³Exponents. A small raised number means multiply the base by itself that many times.
3rd× ÷Multiply and divide. Work left to right, whichever one you meet first.
4th+ −Add and subtract. Work left to right, whichever one you meet first.

The most common mistake

Multiply and divide are equal partners. So are add and subtract. You do not do all the multiplying first. You take them left to right, whichever comes first. In 36 ÷ 6 × 2 you divide first, because the divide sign comes first.

Worked example

Solve: 4³ − 36 ÷ 6 + 2 (9 + 12)

  1. Parentheses first. 9 + 12 = 21

    4³ − 36 ÷ 6 + 2 × 21

  2. Now the exponent. 4³ means 4 × 4 × 4 = 64

    64 − 36 ÷ 6 + 2 × 21

  3. Multiply and divide, left to right. The divide sign comes first. 36 ÷ 6 = 6

    64 − 6 + 2 × 21

  4. Keep going left to right. 2 × 21 = 42

    64 − 6 + 42

  5. Add and subtract, left to right. The minus sign comes first. 64 − 6 = 58

    58 + 42

  6. Last step. 58 + 42 = 100

    100
