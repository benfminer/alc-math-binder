WHICH ONE
DO I NEED?

Perimeter, area and volume all measure a shape. They just measure different things. Start here.

Pick Your Skill

Tell Them Apart

Think about the job you are doing. That tells you which one you need.

You need PERIMETER when you go around the edge

Putting a fence around a yard. Buying trim for the bottom of a wall. Framing a picture. You only care about the outside line.

You need AREA when you cover a flat surface

Buying carpet for a floor. Painting a wall. Laying tile. You care about the whole flat space inside.

You need VOLUME when you fill something up

Filling a box with packing peanuts. Filling a fish tank with water. Loading a moving truck. You care about the space inside a solid object.

Fast check

Around it? Perimeter. Cover it? Area. Fill it? Volume.

Try It Yourself

PERIMETER

The distance all the way around the outside of a flat shape.

What It Is

Imagine walking around the edge of a room without ever cutting across the middle. How far did you walk? That is the perimeter.

Every side counts, even the ones you cannot see a label on. A rectangle has four sides, so you add four numbers.

P = add all the sides

Your answer is measured in plain units, like feet or inches. Nothing gets squared.

Worked Example

A room is 8 feet long and 5 feet wide. How much baseboard do you need to go all the way around?

  1. Write down the length of every side. A rectangle has two long sides and two short sides.

    8   5   8   5

  2. Add the two long sides.

    8 + 8 = 16

  3. Add the two short sides.

    5 + 5 = 10

  4. Add those two answers together.

    16 + 10 = 26 ft

    You need 26 feet of baseboard.

Watch out for this

A rectangle only gives you two labels, but it still has four sides. Do not add just 8 + 5 and stop. That is the most common mistake on this skill.

AREA

The amount of flat space inside a shape.

What It Is

Area is how many squares it takes to cover a shape completely, with no gaps and no overlaps.

Counting squares works, but multiplying is faster. Length times width gives you the same answer.

A = length × width

Because you multiplied two measurements together, your answer is in square units, written like ft² or in².

Worked Example

A room is 8 feet long and 5 feet wide. How much carpet do you need to cover the floor?

  1. Find the length and the width.

    length = 8   width = 5

  2. Put them into the formula.

    A = 8 × 5

  3. Multiply.

    8 × 5 = 40

  4. Add the units. You multiplied two measurements, so the answer is in square feet.

    A = 40 ft²

    You need 40 square feet of carpet.

Watch out for this

Same room, two different answers. The perimeter is 26 ft and the area is 40 ft². Read the question again and ask yourself: am I going around it or covering it?

VOLUME

The amount of space inside a solid object, like a box.

What It Is

Volume is how many little cubes it takes to fill a box all the way up.

Fill the bottom of the box: 5 across by 2 back is 10 cubes. That is one layer. Now stack 3 layers. 10 cubes times 3 layers is 30 cubes.

Multiplying all three measurements at once does the same thing faster.

V = length × width × height

You multiplied three measurements, so the answer is in cubic units, written like in³ or ft³.

Worked Example

This is the same box you just saw. It is 5 inches long, 2 inches wide and 3 inches tall. How much space is inside it?

  1. Write down all three measurements.

    l = 5   w = 2   h = 3

  2. Multiply the first two. This fills the bottom layer.

    5 × 2 = 10

  3. Multiply that by the height. This stacks the layers.

    10 × 3 = 30

  4. Add the units. Three measurements multiplied means cubic inches.

    V = 30 in³

    The box holds 30 cubic inches.

Watch out for this

The order does not matter. 5 × 2 × 3 and 3 × 5 × 2 both give 30. Multiply them in whatever order is easiest for you.
